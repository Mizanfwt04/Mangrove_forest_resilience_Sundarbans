The questions that connect us

What helps a forest thrive—and keep thriving?

Which species and traits sustain ecosystem function? How do trees respond to heat, drought and salinity? And when does a carbon-rich forest become a vulnerable one?

GAINS brings these questions together. The programme builds on research in biodiversity, forest structure and carbon storage, and extends toward continuous tree monitoring and ecological forecasting. Ecological questions guide the technology, from field plots to satellites.

Our purpose

Mission

Address societal and environmental challenges by integrating ecological science, Earth observation, GeoAI, IoT and digital twins. Through CLEAR, BLESS, BEST and TRACE, connect community-led restoration and adaptation, responsible bioeconomy and circular resource systems, sustainable built environments, and transparent carbon and nature accounting. Advance biodiversity protection, climate and disaster resilience, early warning of ecological tipping points, carbon-neutrality pathways, sustainable living and human well-being.

The future we work towards

Vision

A future in which people and nature thrive together: communities are resilient, resources are used responsibly, buildings and cities are sustainable, and environmental decisions are grounded in transparent evidence. Build an international research and innovation institute connecting these aims across forests, coasts, agricultural landscapes and urban systems, advancing equitable, sustainable living and human well-being.

Guiding principles: Ecological integrity, human well-being and equity, community participation, transparent evidence, open methods where possible, responsible innovation, and partnerships across disciplines.

Forest carbon & blue carbon

Resilience & ecological tipping points

Investigate resistance and recovery under drought, heat, cyclones and salinity. Test early-warning signals of ecological tipping points, forest dieback and carbon loss against observed change, linking ecosystem resilience with disaster risk reduction.

The research framework

Many ways to observe.
One connected understanding.

Linking measurements at different scales helps explain how tree-level processes shape forest function—and where predictions need stronger evidence.

01 / OBSERVE

Listen to living trees

Field plots, species inventories, functional traits and proposed IoT / TreeTalker measurements of physiology, growth and microclimate.

02 / MAP

See the whole landscape

Drones, terrestrial and airborne LiDAR, GEDI and satellite imagery connect canopy structure and phenology across spatial scales.

03 / UNDERSTAND

Explain and forecast

GeoAI, time-series analysis and ecological models connect patterns to processes, with field validation and explicit uncertainty.

04 / APPLY

Support better decisions

Evidence for biodiversity conservation, carbon monitoring, restoration and adaptation in natural, urban and agricultural landscapes.

The goal: ecological digital twins updated with new observations to compare management and climate scenarios, investigate tipping-point risks, and support restoration, disaster resilience and carbon-neutrality pathways.
