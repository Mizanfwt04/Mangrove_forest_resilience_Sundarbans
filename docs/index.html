Mission

Theory first. Scale with GeoAI.

We treat AI and remote sensing as tools to test and extend ecological theory across landscapes — not as a substitute for mechanism. Field ecology, traits, and biodiversity remain the foundation.

Ecological theory

Biodiversity & ecosystem functioning

Our work asks how taxonomic diversity, functional diversity, and especially functional composition maintain multiple ecosystem properties and processes — productivity, carbon stocks, resilience, and recovery after disturbance.

Functional composition

Dominant trait strategies (e.g. canopy height, specific leaf area, wood density) often outweigh richness alone in sustaining function (Global Change Biology, 2024).

Structural diversity

Stand structure mediates links from species richness to resilience and recovery capacity (Communications Earth & Environment, 2026).

Co-benefits

Protecting biodiversity and carbon together — nature-based solutions with measurable multifunctionality (Nature Communications, 2021).

Tipping points & CSD

Critical slowing down, autocorrelation, and long-term kNDVI signals to detect loss of resilience under climate extremes.

Methods

Structural equation modeling & GeoAI

Structural equation modeling (SEM) is central: we quantify direct and indirect pathways linking biodiversity, environment, disturbance, and ecosystem properties — then scale those mechanisms with multi-sensor GeoAI.

  • 01

    Structural equation modeling

    Test hypotheses on how traits, diversity, climate, and perturbation jointly shape carbon, resilience, and other processes — with mediated pathways, not only correlations.

  • 02

    Essential Biodiversity Variables

    Species populations, traits, community composition, and ecosystem function as decision-ready monitoring variables.
