About BEST

Better places for people and nature.

BEST brings together sustainable urban planning, buildings, infrastructure and environmental technologies. Its research agenda asks how the places we build can support climate resilience, environmental quality, biodiversity and human well-being.

Working from buildings and neighbourhoods to city-regions, BEST connects the GAINS strengths in ecology, Earth observation and GeoAI with planning and built-environment research. Field measurements and spatial models will help compare practical options for urban development and adaptation.

Focus areas

From buildings to city-regions.

Six connected directions define the platform’s research agenda and future collaborations.

01

Sustainable urban planning

Study land use, urban form, density, housing, mobility and public space to inform inclusive development and adaptation.

02

Healthy urban environments

Assess heat, air pollution, flooding and environmental exposure, including how risks and access to healthier surroundings vary across neighbourhoods.

03

Urban nature & green infrastructure

Connect urban trees, biodiversity, green roofs and blue–green networks with cooling, habitat connectivity, water management and ecosystem services.

04

Smart buildings & infrastructure

Develop research on indoor and outdoor environmental monitoring, thermal comfort and resource use, connecting sensor observations with building and infrastructure performance.

05

GeoAI, IoT & urban digital twins

Bring together GIS, drones, LiDAR, satellites and sensor networks to map urban conditions and develop digital twins for testing planning and adaptation scenarios.

06

Low-carbon development & sustainable technologies

Evaluate opportunities for building retrofits, resource efficiency, material reuse and lower-carbon development, with specialist partners in engineering and life-cycle assessment.

Initial programme direction

Healthy, climate-resilient neighbourhoods.

A proposed programme linking urban form, tree function and environmental exposure to evaluate where changes could make the greatest difference.

Planning & exposure

Understand the neighbourhood

Connect building density, land use, streets and public space with heat, air quality and the distribution of environmental risks.

Ecology & sensing

Measure how urban nature functions

Combine tree physiology, IoT observations, biodiversity surveys and remote sensing to examine the performance of urban green infrastructure.

Scenarios & decisions

Compare pathways for improvement

Evaluate options for trees, green roofs, neighbourhood design and building retrofits against environmental outcomes and community priorities.

The approach

Observe places. Compare possibilities.

A proposed pathway linking environmental measurements, urban form and community priorities with practical planning choices.

  1. ObserveMeasure the built environment, urban nature and environmental conditions.
  2. ConnectRelate spatial patterns to building performance, exposure and community needs.
  3. CompareTest development, retrofit and green-infrastructure scenarios with explicit uncertainty.
  4. EvaluateAssess environmental and social outcomes and refine the approach with partners.

BEST is in development. Its initial focus connects planning, environmental monitoring and urban nature; specialist collaborations will support further work on building performance, engineering and sustainable technologies.

Dr. Kabir connects urban planning and geospatial analysis with research on environmental quality, climate vulnerability and sustainable development.

His work examines land use, neighbourhood air quality and exposure inequality, alongside household livelihoods and adaptation to climate-related hazards. He brings experience in university teaching and academic leadership, urban planning, project management, and environmental and social assessment.
