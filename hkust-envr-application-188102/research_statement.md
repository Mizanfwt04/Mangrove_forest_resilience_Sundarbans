## 1. Research vision

Healthy ecosystems underpin climate resilience, human wellbeing, and sustainable development. Yet biodiversity loss and climate extremes are eroding the **ecosystem services** on which cities and societies depend. My research asks a central question: **How does biodiversity sustain ecosystem functioning and services under environmental change—and how can that knowledge inform effective nature-based solutions?**

I address this question by integrating **field ecology**, **structural equation modelling (SEM)**, **multi-sensor remote sensing** (UAV, TLS/LiDAR, Sentinel, GEDI, Planet), and **GeoAI** to quantify links among biodiversity, carbon, resilience, and tipping-point risk from plot to landscape scale. I treat artificial intelligence and Earth observation as tools to test and scale ecological theory—not as substitutes for mechanistic understanding.

This vision aligns directly with ENVR’s **Biodiversity and Nature-Based Solutions Hub**, BSAP2035, the **2027 biodiversity core curriculum**, and HKUST’s commitment to use its campus as a **living laboratory** for NbS research and education.

## 2. Past research and key findings

### Biodiversity and ecosystem functioning

My work demonstrates that **functional composition**—the identity and traits of dominant species—often matters more than taxonomic or functional diversity alone in maintaining ecosystem properties and processes in mangrove forests (*Global Change Biology*, 2024). In the Sundarbans, **functional composition and structural diversity** jointly enhance forest resilience to environmental stress (*Communications Earth & Environment*, 2026). These findings advance IPBES and GBF framings of biodiversity as a regulator of nature’s contributions to people, not merely a conservation endpoint.

### Ecosystem services: carbon, coastal protection, and multifunctionality

I have quantified spatial variation in **blue-carbon stocks** across salinity zones and vegetation types, and shown **co-benefits of mangrove protection for biodiversity conservation and carbon storage** (*Nature Communications*, 2021; *Wetlands Ecology and Management*, 2014). As co-author on Sanderman et al. (2018), I contributed to the first global 30 m mangrove soil-carbon map—work that supports JREDD+, voluntary carbon markets, and national greenhouse-gas reporting. My research links biodiversity dimensions to carbon services through SEM and machine learning, providing transparent, spatially explicit evidence for policy and NbS design.

### Climate extremes, resilience, and early warning

A growing strand of my programme examines how **cyclones, heat, drought, salinity, and compound extremes** affect recovery trajectories and ecosystem stability. I am developing approaches using long-term vegetation indices (e.g. kNDVI), critical slowing down (CSD), and digital-twin concepts to assess **resilience and early warning of ecological tipping points**—priorities under GBF Target 8 and ENVR’s climate–biodiversity integration agenda.

### GeoAI and multi-scale Earth observation

From NASA Goddard (canopy height mapping) to HKU (Sentinel–GEDI–TanDEM-X fusion) and PolyU (UAV/TLS for mangrove structure), I have built a consistent record of fusing field inventory with remote sensing and ML for **carbon MRV**, functional trait mapping, and vegetation dynamics. This technical capacity supports ENVR’s emphasis on data science and the hub’s goal of making biodiversity “visible, understandable, and actionable.”

## 3. Research programme at HKUST (next five years)

I propose an integrated programme organised around three pillars that map onto the Biodiversity & NbS Hub.

### Pillar A — Biodiversity, ecosystem services, and NbS in coastal–terrestrial systems

**Objective:** Quantify how biodiversity supports regulating and supporting services in forests, mangroves, urban greenspace, and restored habitats.

**Approach:** Combine permanent plots, TLS/LiDAR, and satellite time series with SEM and explainable GeoAI to model biodiversity–function–service pathways.

**HK relevance:** Apply methods to HKUST’s managed coastline, slope vegetation, and urban green infrastructure; collaborate with AFCD-aligned curriculum and BSAP2035 priorities.

**Outputs:** High-impact publications; open datasets and code; biodiversity–carbon assessment protocols for NbS projects.

### Pillar B — Climate extremes, resilience, and tipping-point science for NbS

**Objective:** Assess how NbS perform under compound climate extremes and identify early warning signals of ecosystem degradation.

**Approach:** Long-term remote sensing, CSD analytics, and field monitoring of post-extreme recovery; link to digital-twin forest/coastal models.

**HK relevance:** Evaluate resilience of campus and regional NbS (mangrove restoration, eco-engineered shorelines, urban trees) under typhoons, marine heatwaves, and heat stress—complementing CARE2026 and My Climate Risk Hub activities.

**Outputs:** Resilience indicators for NbS standards; policy briefs on climate-adaptive restoration design.

### Pillar C — Transparent carbon MRV and decision support for Net Zero

**Objective:** Deliver scientifically rigorous, spatially explicit carbon and biodiversity MRV for campuses, jurisdictions, and NbS portfolios.

**Approach:** Integrate UAV/LiDAR, GEDI, and multispectral/radar fusion with ML and uncertainty quantification; support JREDD+ and voluntary carbon integrity.

**HK relevance:** Partner with HKUST’s Sustainability/Net Zero Office on campus carbon accounting and offset science; contribute to Hong Kong’s green-finance and NbS financing dialogue.

**Funding strategy:** RGC General Research Fund and Collaborative Research Fund; Environment and Conservation Fund; NSFC/Hong Kong joint schemes; EU/UK bilateral programmes; industry and NGO partnerships on MRV and restoration.
